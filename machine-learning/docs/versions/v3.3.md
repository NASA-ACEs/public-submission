# v3.3 (2026-11-15, tag `model-v3.3`)

One change from v3.2.1: the large-fire model sees the surroundings of the cell. LFMC models, fuel age, NISAR
terms and the reference of `risk_index` are unchanged.

## The surroundings of the cell

v3.2 showed that the largest single input was how much of the 200 m cell is built up. Whether an ignition grows
to 400 ha is decided by what lies around it, so v3.3 adds 17 inputs computed on the 200 m California grid
(`ml.context`):

- means over discs of 1 km and 3 km (5 and 15 cells) of: built-up share, agriculture share, burnable share, total
  fuel load, canopy cover, forest share and shrub share (sums of the vegetation classes), from the LANDFIRE
  release valid on the day;
- terrain: standard deviation of elevation within 1 km, and the cell's elevation minus the mean within 1 km and
  3 km.

No monotone constraints. A map of any AOI takes them from the California grid at the centres of its cells, so the
discs are those of training. Statewide map: 38 s more.

The set and the acceptance rule were written before the run (`docs/research/landscape-context.md`), the same rule
as for vegetation type: on 2025–2026 the paired ROC-AUC gain for fires ≥ 400 ha has an interval above zero and
PR-AUC does not fall; PR-AUC in unseen years is not significantly worse. Met, and for the first time the repo's
own regression test says "better".

**What carries it.** The 17 inputs hold 41 % of the trees' gain; the vegetation shares of the cell itself fall
from 22 % to 8 %, the cell's fuel and terrain hold 18 %, weather and its sums 32 %. The largest inputs are now
roughness of the terrain within 1 km (6.4 %), VPD (6.2 %), the built-up share within 3 km (6.2 %) and within 1 km
(6.0 %), and maximum wind (5.9 %). In the ablations of `metrics_full.json` the surroundings add +0.011
[0.001, 0.021] PR-AUC and +0.018 [0.011, 0.025] ROC-AUC; with them in the model, removing the cell's own fuel and
terrain or its vegetation shares no longer costs anything (+0.0001 [−0.008, 0.007]; −0.003 [−0.008, 0.001]).

## Limits

- **The years 2025–2026 have now decided two changes** (vegetation type, then this). They are no longer an
  untouched test for whatever comes next.
- The unseen-year PR-AUC interval starts at 0.0002 and refitting with five other seeds moves PR-AUC by 0.0025;
  the ROC-AUC gain is not narrow.
- In 2022–2024 alone PR-AUC is lower (0.108 → 0.098 before fuel age; paired −0.009 [−0.032, 0.023]) while ROC-AUC
  rises (0.878 → 0.900). In 2025 alone no change for ≥ 400 ha excludes zero; 2026 carries the ROC-AUC result.
- "1 km" and "3 km" are 5 and 15 cells of the equal-area grid: an ellipse of about 0.96 × 1.05 km at 34° N.
- The 2016 rows have terrain surroundings only; their fold is scored without the dated ones (as in v3.2).
- The dryness z-scores, through which the NISAR term enters `risk_index`, still add nothing measurable
  (PR-AUC −0.006 [−0.014, 0.001], ROC-AUC −0.001 [−0.003, 0.001]).

## v3.3.1 (tag `model-v3.3.1`): the land and the weather, separately

The same trees and probabilities. Training now records the "ordinary day": each of the model's 21 weather inputs
at its median over the training ignitions (`ordinary_day` in `meta.json`). The map predicts a second time with
those values and writes

- `susceptibility_index`: the rank of that probability, what the land contributes (terrain, fuels, vegetation,
  surroundings, fuel age). Until now this file was a copy of `risk_index`;
- `weather_factor`: the odds of the day divided by the odds of the ordinary day. Above 1 the day's weather raises
  the risk of the cell.

On the ignitions of 2025–2026 (80 large fires; `v3.3.1/land_and_weather_2025_2026.json`) the two parts are nearly
independent (rank correlation −0.16) and each ranks large fires alone: land ROC-AUC 0.78, weather factor 0.83,
together 0.90. A factor of 2 or more: 7.3 % of the ignitions and 38 of the 80 large fires; below 0.5: 26 % of the
ignitions and 2 large fires.

| Fire | `p_large_fire` | Land: rank | Weather factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garnet, 2025-08-24 | 60.9 % | 0.94 | × 28 |
| Hughes, 2025-01-22 | 15.2 % | 0.52 | × 22 |
| Eaton, 2025-01-07 | 4.8 % | 0.41 | × 8.9 |
| Palisades, 2025-01-07 | 2.2 % | 0.30 | × 6.0 |
| Gifford, 2025-08-01 | 0.8 % | 0.52 | × 1.0 |

The Los Angeles fires of January 2025 started on land the model ranks as ordinary or below (the edge of the city)
on a day whose weather multiplied the odds by 6 and 9. Gifford, the largest fire of the two years and the model's
worst miss among the large ones, started on an ordinary day: nothing in the weather inputs set it apart.

The decomposition is a description of the model, not a second model: the ordinary day is a set of medians, not a
day that occurred, and what the trees do with combinations of land and weather is shared between the two parts.

## v3.3.2 (tag `model-v3.3.2`): fixes from an independent review

The models are those of v3.3.1, file for file. An independent review of the night's changes
(`git diff model-v3.1 model-v3.3.1 -- src tests`) found no error in the results and these weak points, now fixed:

- on an AOI other than `ca`, a cell whose centre falls in a `ca` cell without data (the coast) had no
  surroundings, a combination the models know only from the rows of 2016: 140 of 609,840 cells of the Santa
  Monica map, median `risk_index` 0.14 where 0.38 is right. They now take the nearest value. Nothing else in that
  map changes, and the statewide map is the same;
- the 2016 fold is scored without fuel layers also when a few rows without them sit among the training rows
  (one such row would have switched the protocol off without a word; the current table has none);
- table and map choose LANDFIRE releases by the same rule for the surroundings; a release without a validity
  date in `data.LANDFIRE_FROM` is an error instead of being used for every row; terrain never depends on the
  release;
- the rank reference records when it falls back to all ignitions, and the bundle's note names the years.

What the review confirmed by running: surroundings in the table equal the code's on real data; the disc mean
equals a loop to 5e-6; the `ca` values read for `smm` are aligned (shifting by one cell is worse); every row of
the unseen-year protocol is scored once per seed; the map restores the weather columns before later layers use
them; no large fire among the 1,031 ignitions of October to December 2022, when a new release starts.

A sweep of seven model settings on the 71 features, with its rule written first, changed nothing
(`docs/research/settings-sweep.md`).
