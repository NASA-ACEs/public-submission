# v3.1 (2026-11-15, tag `model-v3.1`)

Two changes from v3; the trees and the LFMC models are those of v3.

## Fuel age depends on what burned

Study `docs/research/fuel-age-by-vegetation.md` (410 later fires against earlier scars, and ignitions 2016–2026):

- every scar protects while it is young: no ignition inside a scar younger than 2 years became a large fire in
  2016–2026 (632 ignitions, 12.6 expected); cells of scars younger than 2 years burned 0.33 [0.24, 0.43] times as
  often as neighbouring land when a later large fire reached them, in every period including 2025–2026;
- former forest stays protected for about 7 years (0.26–0.46); shrub for about 2 years, then about 0.8; grass and
  open land not after 2 years (ratio above 1);
- the single curve of v3, fading over 2 years for all vegetation, understated risk in old scars on open land in
  2025–2026: 7 of the 10 large fires in scars of 3.5–10 years started where canopy cover is below 10 %, against
  2.3 expected.

v3.1: the odds of a large fire are multiplied by exp(−β·exp(−years)) in scars younger than 3.5 years (β = 12,
fitted on 2016–2024: 0.01 after one year, 0.20 after two, 0.55 after three, 1 from 3.5) and, where the fuel model
of the oldest LANDFIRE release was a forest model, stay lowered for 7 years (0.41 after four years, 0.63 after
six). Spread permeability fades with a time constant of 7.0 years in former forest, 2.3 in shrub, 1.3 in grass.
The forest extension was defined after seeing the tests of that study.

Regression test against v3 (`compare_with_v3_full.json`): no difference in either system. The change makes the
model cautious where the earlier form was not.

## NISAR SME2 against the 2026 fire season: the pre-registered test, final

`docs/research/nisar-fire-season.md`. Scenes to 2026-10-20; 2,113 ignitions of June–October 2026 with the
predictor, 213 fires ≥ 4 ha.

| Test | Result | Passed |
|---|---|---|
| (1) AUC of the SME2 anomaly alone, ≥ 4 ha | 0.550 [0.520, 0.576]; SMAP's anomalies at the same ignitions 0.447 and 0.463 | yes |
| (2) Odds ratio per z-unit with weather, day of season and SMAP controlled | 0.89 [0.82, 0.98] | yes |
| (3) Resolution: the part finer than 9 km added to the SMAP root-zone anomaly | +0.027 [−0.012, 0.059] | no (surface: +0.030 [0.002, 0.058]) |
| (4) Burned against a ring outside, 45 perimeters | 21 drier inside, 24 wetter; p = 0.77 | no |
| (5) The map's fine-scale term through the model | ROC-AUC +0.004 [0.0005, 0.008]; PR-AUC +0.0005 [−0.003, 0.004] | |

Decision by the rule fixed in advance: **no claim of skill for the fine-scale NISAR term**; it stays in the map as
a small, experimental contribution. What did pass is the anomaly itself: a pixel drier than its own NISAR record
had more fires that grew, beyond weather and beyond SMAP. Added after the protocol, it also holds with the full
model controlled (odds ratio 0.84 [0.76, 0.90] for ≥ 4 ha). On that basis the bundle carries **experimental
layers** `sme2_anomaly`, `p_large_fire_sme2`, `risk_index_sme2`: the odds scaled by 0.84 per unit of the anomaly.
The coefficient comes from the one season NISAR has; `risk_index` does not use it.
