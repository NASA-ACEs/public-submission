# v3.2 (2026-11-15, tag `model-v3.2`)

Two changes from v3.1: the large-fire model sees what covers the cell, and the 2016 fold of the unseen-year
protocol is scored properly. The LFMC models, the fuel-age modifier and the NISAR terms are those of v3.1.

## Vegetation type of the cell

Sixteen inputs: the share of the 200 m cell under each class of LANDFIRE existing vegetation (chaparral, coastal
scrub, sagebrush, desert scrub, other shrub, oak woodland, other hardwood, conifer–oak, dry conifer, mesic
conifer, juniper–pinyon, grass, riparian, sparse, agriculture, **developed**), from the release valid on the day
(collection `landfire-evt`). No monotone constraints. Only in the large-fire model: for LFMC the same shares did
not help (`docs/research/vegetation-type.md`).

The first study (`vegetation-type.md`) found a gain in ROC-AUC and none in PR-AUC, with no independent check.
The check now exists, and the rule was written down before it was run
(`docs/research/vegetation-type-out-of-time.md`): accept only if, on the ignitions of 2025–2026, the paired gain
in ROC-AUC for fires ≥ 400 ha has an interval above zero and PR-AUC does not fall, and PR-AUC in unseen years is
not significantly worse.

| 2025–2026, 13,296 ignitions (paired, bootstrap over weeks) | Events | PR-AUC, v3.1 → v3.2 | ROC-AUC, v3.1 → v3.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≥ 400 ha | 80 | 0.0594 → 0.0668 (+0.0074 [−0.030, 0.023]) | 0.843 → 0.871 (**+0.028 [0.011, 0.047]**) |
| ≥ 40 ha | 239 | 0.0689 → 0.0795 (+0.011 [−0.004, 0.018]) | 0.758 → 0.785 (**+0.026 [0.018, 0.036]**) |
| ≥ 4 ha | 875 | 0.1237 → 0.1362 (**+0.013 [0.007, 0.018]**) | 0.674 → 0.697 (**+0.023 [0.019, 0.028]**) |

Large fires among the top 1 / 5 / 10 / 20 % of the ignitions of 2025–2026: 11 / 38 / 50 / 70 % → 15 / 38 / 54 /
74 %. Both years gain (ROC-AUC 0.874 → 0.895 in 2025, 0.806 → 0.842 in 2026). Expected large fires: 86 against
80 observed.

Unseen years, both sides scored by the corrected protocol below (74,568 ignitions, 477 large): PR-AUC 0.0703 →
0.0712 (+0.0009 [−0.0040, 0.0082]), ROC-AUC 0.861 → 0.867 (**+0.0065 [0.0022, 0.0117]**), large fires in the top
10 %: 60 → 61 %. On 2017–2024 alone, where the scoring did not change (431 large): PR-AUC 0.0801 → 0.0814
(+0.0013 [−0.0057, 0.0097]), ROC-AUC 0.862 → 0.870 (+0.0071 [0.0026, 0.0129]).

So the rule is met. By the repo's main rule (a PR-AUC gain whose interval excludes zero) vegetation type is
still "no difference" for fires ≥ 400 ha; what it improves is the ranking over the whole range (ROC-AUC, every
size class, both protocols) and the ranking of fires ≥ 4 ha.

**What carries it.** The shares hold 22 % of the trees' gain, and `evt_share_developed` alone 12 %: it is the
largest single input, ahead of slope (12 %) and maximum wind (7 %). Of the ignitions of 2017–2024:

| Built-up share of the cell | Ignitions | Became ≥ 400 ha |
|---|---|---|
| none | 10,618 | 244 (2.30 %) |
| up to 10 % | 2,596 | 24 (0.92 %) |
| 10–50 % | 16,377 | 87 (0.53 %) |
| over half | 36,988 | 76 (0.21 %) |

More than half of all ignitions start in cells that are mostly built up, and they become large fires one-eleventh
as often as ignitions on land with no buildings. Fuel loads and the burnable share did not tell the model this as
clearly. It is a statement about fire size, not about consequence: a fire near buildings is less likely to grow
and costs more when it does. Part of it is how quickly fires near towns are reached, not fuel alone.

## The 2016 fold

The LANDFIRE fuel, canopy and vegetation layers are used from the release valid on the day, and the first one is
valid from 2017-01-01. The 7,892 ignitions of 2016 therefore have terrain and no fuel inputs. Until v3.1 the 2016
fold was predicted by trees that had never seen a missing value in those inputs, so what they did with one was
arbitrary (the fold's mean prediction was 3.2 ‰ against 5.8 ‰ observed; with the vegetation shares added it moved
between 1.5 and 5.7 ‰ from one run to another). From v3.2 such rows are scored by models fitted without those
layers (`ml.train.fire_cv`); folds whose training rows include 2016 are unchanged, and so is the saved model,
which is fitted on all years and learns where to send the missing values.

For the features of v3.1 the corrected scoring gives PR-AUC 0.0703 where 0.0748 was reported (−0.0045 [−0.0120,
0.0026]), ROC-AUC 0.861 for 0.856, and 85 expected large fires in 2025–2026 for 92 (80 observed), because the
calibration is fitted on these predictions. **Figures of earlier versions use the earlier scoring; compare v3.2
with the corrected v3.1 row.** In the 2016 fold itself: ROC-AUC 0.831 → 0.849.

## Data

`landfire-evt` is not yet a dataset of fuel-lake's `main`. On the server it is registered in
`~/spaceapps/lanes/ML` for `ca` (shares at 200 m) and `smm` (0/1 layers at 30 m, averaged over 200 m windows by
`ml.grid`), releases LF 2016, 2022 and 2025, with a copy of fuel-lake's configuration that adds the dataset
(`FUELLAKE_CONFIG_DIR=~/spaceapps/ml-work/config`). Without the collection the shares are left out and training
gives the trees of v3.1.

## v3.2.1 (tag `model-v3.2.1`): what `risk_index` ranks against

The same trees, calibration and probabilities. `risk_index` was the rank of a cell's probability among the
predictions for all ignitions of 2016–2024. More than half of those start in cells that are mostly built up and
have low probabilities (median 0.21 %), so on the map of 2026-10-20 45 % of California's burnable land, and 53 %
of the Santa Monica Mountains, was in the top fifth: the layer the app opens with showed almost nothing. From
v3.2.1 the reference is the 13,333 ignitions of 2017–2024 in cells at most 10 % built up (268 large; median
probability 0.82 %). On the same day the five classes hold 20 / 30 / 26 / 17 / 8 % of the state's burnable land.
`result.json` carries `rank_reference`. Maps of v3.2.1 are not comparable in `risk_index` and `risk_class` with
earlier bundles; `p_large_fire` is.
